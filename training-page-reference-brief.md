# Corporate training page reference brief

*Prepared 29 August 2026. The requested `https://listai.com/training` page timed out and returned no indexed result. The page observations below therefore use the root checkout's `/training` route as a fallback. That route is branded Elyst AI and points to `elystai.com`; confirm the intended domain before implementing changes.*

## What the current page does well

- The proposition is strong: “AI training built around the work your team already does.”
- The dithered green hero visual gives the page a distinctive brand anchor.
- “Discover → Design → Deliver → Follow through” is a credible delivery spine.
- The page correctly mentions sensitive information, human review, and the limits of AI.
- “We do not promise productivity gains” is more trustworthy than an unsupported ROI claim.

## What is holding it back

The page is visually calm but becomes a sequence of large, lightly populated sections: bullets for audience, outcomes, formats, and modules. The buyer still cannot see a real session, a concrete deliverable, a typical team size, or what happens after the room. The proof area is an unresolved placeholder in the accessible build, and the global “Book an audit call” button conflicts with the training CTA. The modules also read like a generic syllabus; they are less useful than examples of work people will actually practise.

Keep the same core promise, but make relevance obvious by role. |

| [IDEO U Team Learning](https://www.ideou.com/pages/team-training) | Makes learning-by-doing explicit and supplies activities, feedback, a team guide, and a way to manage completion. | Name the artefacts participants leave with: one practised workflow, a prompt/checklist, safe-use rules, and a next-step plan. |
| [Harvard Business School Executive Education](https://www.exed.hbs.edu/the-learning-experience) | Uses cases, simulations, coaching, discussion groups, and blended delivery to make learning social and applied. | Add one anonymised case or session story and explain how participants compare, critique, and improve work together. |
| [Coursera for Business](https://www.coursera.org/business/) | Maps learning to roles, uses hands-on practice/simulations, and makes progress visible through baselines and reporting. | Offer a lightweight pre-session baseline and one agreed follow-up signal; do not copy the platform complexity. |

## Recommended page changes, in order

1. **Move proof directly below the hero.** Use the real Aug 18 session if permission and facts are confirmed: audience mix, format, duration, tools, exercises, outputs, and one approved participant quote. An anonymised session card is enough; invented client logos are not.
2. **Replace generic bullets with tangible takeaways.** For example: a completed real-work exercise, a reusable prompt or checklist, agreed data boundaries, a human-review step, and a 7–14-day application plan.
3. **Add four example workflows.** Use cross-functional examples such as meeting-to-decision brief, research-to-summary, proposal/RFQ response, and customer or operations document review. Label them as examples adapted to the team’s tools.
4. **Make the delivery model concrete.** Show pre-session discovery, live practice, a short debrief, and follow-up. State indicative duration, participant range, remote/on-site options, and what the client supplies.
5. **Add a trust block.** Explain what participants should not paste into tools, where human approval remains mandatory, and how tool access or permissions affect the session.
6. **Fix the conversion path.** On this page, every primary CTA should lead to a training-fit conversation, not an audit call. Ask for role mix, team size, tools, work to improve, and preferred format.
7. **Finish or remove the certificate FAQ.** A blank answer weakens trust. Also answer preparation, pricing approach, participant limits, follow-up support, and whether the session can use company tools.
8. **Add regional work examples without stereotyping.** For GCC-facing buyers, examples can include WhatsApp-to-CRM lead handling, multilingual customer context, real-estate qualification, logistics/RFQ work, and document review. Only promise languages, tools, or data handling that Elyst can actually deliver.

## Keep, change, remove

- **Keep:** the headline, distinctive hero, safety language, realistic tone, and four-stage method.
- **Change:** “Who it is for,” “What you get,” “Formats,” and “Modules” from lists into cards, examples, and explicit outputs.
- **Remove or demote:** the long generic module list and the empty proof placeholder. Training buyers need confidence in fit and transfer before curriculum breadth.

The target page should make one promise easy to believe: *your team will practise a relevant piece of work, understand the limits and safeguards, and leave with something usable and a clear next step.*
