# Optional future /work or /case-studies reference brief

*Decision: useful after the first permissioned implementation case, but not a launch requirement today. The current evidence gap should be solved before adding another navigation item.*

## Why this page may become necessary

The homepage, Services page, About page, and Training page can explain the model, but a buyer still needs one place to inspect actual work. A proof page becomes worthwhile when Elyst has at least one permissioned implementation case and one permissioned training story. Until then, a transparent proof block is better than an empty portfolio page.

## Reference points worth borrowing

| Reference | Strong pattern | Apply to Elyst |
|---|---|---|
| [Fourlines: Pentagon Real Estate](https://www.fourlinesagency.com/work/pentagon-real-estate) | Challenge, precise brief, existing-stack build, deployment, and explicit human entry point. | Show the workflow and decision boundary, not just the finished interface. |
| [Upteky: UAE logistics](https://upteky.com/case-studies/uae-logistics-ai-crm-lead-reactivation/) | Separates challenge, what was built, and results with operational measures. | Use baseline → intervention → measured change. Mark client-reported figures as client-reported. |
| [Deloitte: vision to value](https://www.deloitte.com/global/en/what-we-do/case-studies-collection/harnessing-ai-to-shape-tomorrows-world.html) | Explains the client situation, capability built, roadmap, and limits of the outcome. | Include what changed in the client’s operating model and what remains future work. |
| [Polara Venture Studio](https://www.polaraventurestudio.com/) | Uses live products as proof that the method reaches production. | Add a “what is live” section only for systems Elyst can verify and name or anonymise with permission. |

## Recommended case structure

1. Client/sector/location, only with permission.
2. The workflow and measurable baseline.
3. What Elyst changed and what stayed human.
4. Tools or systems used, at the level the client approves.
5. Evidence: time, volume, quality, adoption, or another agreed measure.
6. Handover: owner, documentation, access, limits, and support.
7. What the case does not prove.

Do not build this page to fill a menu. Build it when it can reduce buyer uncertainty.
