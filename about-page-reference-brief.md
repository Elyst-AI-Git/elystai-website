# About page reference brief

*Prepared 29 August 2026. The live listai.com page was not reachable, so this audit uses the current working /about route and the approved website-page brief. No site code was changed.*

## Current page read

The working page uses the same split dither hero as the other marketing pages, followed by “Why Elyst exists,” two simple founder cards, operating principles, a preview-only company placeholder, and an audit CTA. The typography, spacing, dark navigation, soft surfaces, and footer are coherent. The page is currently the least differentiated visually and the least complete in content: it has no founder photos or biographies in the rendered component, and the hero still says “A team of two…” rather than the approved fixed title “Your AI partner.”

The page should answer four trust questions quickly:

- Who will actually do the work?
- Why do they understand the problem?
- How do they make decisions and handle risk?
- Where is the company based, and how does delivery work?

## Reference points worth borrowing

| Reference | What it does well | Apply to Elyst |
|---|---|---|
| [BCG: Purpose and People](https://www.bcg.com/about/purpose-people) | Combines a concise purpose statement, named principles, leadership profiles, locations, and scale without hiding behind generic “passion” language. | Keep the principles short, but connect each one to client behaviour: what Elyst checks, refuses, measures, and hands over. |
| [Principle Global: About](https://www.principleglobal.com/en-gb/about) | Shows named regional leadership, a clear origin story, and culture values that are observable in how the company operates. | Explain the two founders’ actual division of responsibility and the Kerala-to-GCC delivery model only where it is true and verifiable. |
| [Polara Venture Studio, Kochi](https://www.polaraventurestudio.com/) | Uses a local company story plus live products to make “we build real systems” credible. | Add a small “what we have built or run” block when permissioned evidence exists. Local provenance matters more than invented scale. |
| [AI Workshop, UAE](https://aiworkshop.ae/) | Separates business, technical, and human capability, then makes the founder’s credentials and delivery geography visible. | Show Shirin’s discovery/adoption role and Nihal’s technical/handover role as complementary capabilities. Publish only verified bios and credentials. |

## Recommended changes, in order

1. **Use the fixed title “Your AI partner.”** The next line should explain the real operating promise: audit the work, build around what matters, train the team, and leave ownership behind.
2. **Replace placeholder founder cards with evidence-backed founder strips.** Use real photos, names, role lines, short bios, LinkedIn links, and a plain description of what each person owns in delivery. Do not publish the draft bio or “first” claim until verified.
3. **Make “How we operate” concrete.** Keep the five principles, but give each one a one-line implication: diagnose before building, keep the system bounded, retain human review, measure the workflow, and say no when AI is not the answer.
4. **Add company facts without overexposing personal details.** Include legal entity, operating city/country, domain email, company LinkedIn, and whether delivery is remote, on-site, or both. Do not add a residential address.
5. **Add a truthful regional sentence.** Kerala is the company’s base. GCC delivery can be stated as an operating focus only if Elyst can actually support the claimed location, travel, and delivery model.
6. **Differentiate the visual role of the page.** Keep the dither for the homepage. Use founder portraits and a horizontal delivery/ownership strip here; the About page should feel human and accountable, not like another service landing page.
7. **Add one small proof of people.** A permissioned training session, client quote, or anonymised artefact is enough for now. Do not turn the page into a logo wall.
8. **Keep the CTA low-pressure.** “Book an audit call” is right, but the page should first make the two-person model feel selective and credible rather than implying unlimited capacity.

The About page should make this believable: *the people on this page are the people who will understand the workflow, build the system, and stay accountable through handover.*
